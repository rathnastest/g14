## History & Heritage

- Ohlone Roots: The region was originally inhabited by the Chochenyo Ohlone Native Americans for thousands of years.
- Spanish & Mexican Era: Explored by the Spanish in 1772, the land later became part of the Mexican land grant Rancho San Leandro, awarded to José Joaquín Estudillo in 1842.
- Agricultural Hub: Founded during the California Gold Rush, San Leandro became famous for its fertile soil, earning a reputation for its extensive cherry orchards and annual flower production. It served as the original county seat of Alameda County from 1855 to 1872.
- Industrial Transition: During the mid-20th century, the city transitioned into a major manufacturing and food-processing hub, famously hosting large operations for companies like [Ghirardelli Chocolate Company](https://www.ghirardelli.com/) and Caterpillar.

---

## Economy & Infrastructure

- Lit San Leandro: A defining feature of the city's modern infrastructure is its municipal high-speed fiber-optic network, offering lightning-fast internet speeds to local businesses and tech startups.
- Key Industries: Modern economy centers around healthcare, technology, advanced manufacturing, and retail food production.
- Major Employers: Top employers include the San Leandro Unified School District, Kaiser Permanente Medical Center, and various medical and logistics hubs.

---

## Culture & Community

- The Cherry Festival: Celebrating its deep agricultural roots, the city has hosted the historic San Leandro Cherry Festival since 1909, drawing thousands of visitors each summer.
- Points of Interest:
- San Leandro Marina: Provides group picnic areas, trails, and scenic waterfront views at Marina Park.
  - Casa Peralta: A historic, Spanish-revival home that serves as a window into the city's early California history.
  - Craft Beer Scene: The city is a recognized destination for craft beer lovers, anchoring several popular local breweries.
- Transportation: The city is highly accessible via major freeways (I-880 and I-580) and features two BART (Bay Area Rapid Transit) stations, making commuting across the Bay Area efficient.
